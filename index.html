Software Patents: Important Legal Considerations

Software patents are an important but complex area of intellectual property law. In the United States, an invention must satisfy several requirements before it can receive patent protection.

One major consideration is patent eligibility under Section 101 of the U.S. Patent Act. Abstract ideas, mathematical concepts, and certain methods of organizing human activity are not automatically patentable merely because they are implemented using a computer.

For example, a basic business method performed through ordinary software may face eligibility challenges. By contrast, a software invention that improves computer functionality or solves a specific technical problem may present a stronger basis for patent eligibility, depending on its details.

Other important requirements include:

Novelty

The claimed invention must meet the applicable requirements for being new.

Non-obviousness

The invention must not be an obvious variation of existing technology under the relevant legal standard.

Utility

The invention must have a specific and legally recognized useful application.

Written description

The application must adequately describe the invention.

Enablement

The description must provide sufficient information for a person skilled in the relevant field to make and use the invention.

Careful patent drafting is essential because the quality of the application can influence how the invention is examined and the scope of protection that may eventually be available.

AI Patent Services and Emerging Technology

Artificial intelligence introduces additional intellectual property questions. AI systems can analyze large datasets, recognize patterns, generate predictions, automate decisions, and support complex technical processes.

As AI technology develops, companies increasingly seek to protect innovations involving machine learning, neural networks, natural language processing, computer vision, and intelligent automation.

An AI-related patent evaluation may examine the specific technical contribution of an invention rather than simply describing it as an artificial intelligence product.

For example, a company developing an AI-powered medical image analysis platform may have several distinct technical components. These could include image-processing methods, model architecture, data-handling techniques, or improvements to computing performance. Each component may require separate consideration.

AI inventors should also understand that, under current United States patent law, inventors must be natural persons. An AI system cannot independently be named as an inventor. Human contributions to conception are therefore important when determining inventorship.

The Importance of Prior-Art Research

Before filing a software or AI patent application, applicants should understand what already exists in the relevant technology field. This is where prior-art research becomes especially important.

Prior art may include previously published patents, academic research, technical papers, product documentation, public demonstrations, and other disclosures relevant to the invention.

A search can help identify similar inventions and potential challenges to novelty or non-obviousness. It can also help applicants understand how their technical contribution differs from existing solutions.

However, a basic internet search is not a substitute for a professional patentability analysis. Patent claims can involve technical distinctions that are not obvious from product descriptions or general search results.

For this reason, inventors may benefit from discussing their technology with a qualified patent professional before submitting an application.

Software Patents vs. Copyright and Trade Secrets

Patents are not the only way to protect software-related intellectual property. Copyright and trade secret protection may also be relevant, depending on the type of asset and the company's business objectives.

Protection TypeWhat It ProtectsMain Consideration
PatentQualifying inventions and claimed technical methodsRequires an application and examination
CopyrightOriginal software code and other qualifying creative expressionDoes not generally protect underlying ideas or functionality
Trade secretValuable confidential business or technical informationRequires reasonable measures to maintain secrecy
TrademarkBrand names, logos, and source identifiersProtects brand identity rather than technical functionality

A company may use more than one form of protection. For example, a business could seek a patent for a qualifying technical process, use copyright to protect its original source code, and maintain confidential model information as a trade secret.

Choosing the right combination depends on the technology, disclosure risks, commercial strategy, and applicable legal requirements.

Common Mistakes to Avoid When Protecting AI and Software Inventions

Technology businesses sometimes make avoidable mistakes when approaching patent protection. One common problem is publicly disclosing an invention before discussing the potential consequences with a patent professional. Public disclosures can affect patent rights, and the rules differ between jurisdictions.

Another mistake is describing a software product too generally. A patent application should explain the actual technical contribution rather than relying on broad statements about automation, intelligence, or improved efficiency.

Businesses should also avoid assuming that every AI-generated result or software feature can be patented. Eligibility and inventorship requirements must be evaluated under the relevant law.

Finally, companies should not overlook international protection. Patent rights are territorial, meaning a patent granted in one country does not automatically provide protection worldwide.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

1. What are Sherinian Law’s software and AI patent services?

Confirm the firm's current service offerings directly before engaging.

2. Can software be patented in the United States?

Yes, certain software-related inventions can receive patent protection if they satisfy applicable eligibility, novelty, non-obviousness, utility, and disclosure requirements. Abstract ideas implemented on a generic computer may face eligibility challenges.

3. Are artificial intelligence inventions patentable?

Some AI-related inventions may qualify for patent protection when they satisfy the relevant legal requirements. The technical contribution, implementation, and claimed invention are important considerations.

4. Can an AI system be listed as a patent inventor?

Under current United States patent law, inventors must be natural persons. An AI system cannot independently be named as an inventor, although AI tools may assist human inventors during development.

5. Why is a prior-art search important?

A prior-art search helps identify existing patents, publications, and other disclosures that may affect whether an invention qualifies for patent protection. It can also help applicants understand how their invention differs from existing technology.

6. How long does a software patent application take?

The timeline varies depending on the technology, application quality, patent office workload, and examination process. Some applications require multiple responses and amendments before reaching a final decision.

7. What information is needed to prepare a software patent application?

Applicants may need technical descriptions, system diagrams, development details, explanations of the problem being solved, implementation examples, and information about the human inventors.

8. What is the difference between a software patent and software copyright?

A software patent may protect qualifying claimed inventions or technical methods. Copyright generally protects the original expression of software, such as source code, rather than the underlying idea or functionality.

9. Can a startup benefit from AI patent protection?

Potentially. Patent protection may help a startup protect qualifying technical innovations, support licensing discussions, and strengthen its intellectual property portfolio. However, the costs and commercial benefits should be evaluated carefully.

10. Do software patents provide worldwide protection?

No. Patent rights are territorial. Applicants generally need to pursue protection in the countries or regions where they want patent rights, subject to applicable international filing procedures.

11. Can a company protect an AI algorithm as a trade secret?

Potentially, if the information has commercial value because it is secret and the company takes reasonable steps to maintain its confidentiality. Trade secret protection does not prevent independent development or lawful reverse engineering in circumstances permitted by applicable law.

12. When should a business contact a patent attorney?

A business should consider speaking with a patent attorney before publicly disclosing a potentially valuable invention, filing an application, licensing technology, or making important intellectual property decisions.
